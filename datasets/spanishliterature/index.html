    Spanish-language literature¶

    ests.datasets.SpanishLiterature

    Description¶

    A collection of Spanish-language literature in the public domain: 150 works by 33 authors from Spain, Latin America and the Philippines, from Cervantes to the 1920s, 65 million characters (about 11 million words) in four genres:

    Genre Key Authors Works
    Prose prose Pérez Galdós (21), Blasco Ibáñez (15), Palacio Valdés (13), Pardo Bazán (13), Pereda (8), Unamuno (6), Valera (6), Valle-Inclán (5), Clarín (3), Payró (3), Alarcón (2), Cervantes (2), Martí (2), Rizal (2), Darío, Larra, Lugones, Palma, Quevedo, Quiroga 107
    Poetry poems Darío (5), Hernández (2), Machado, Rosalía de Castro, Zorrilla 10
    Drama drama Valle-Inclán (4), Arniches (3), Ruiz de Alarcón (3), Lope de Vega (2), Cervantes, Dicenta, Moratín, Zorrilla 16
    Publicism publicism Unamuno (3), Darío (2), Ingenieros (2), Ramón y Cajal (2), Alarcón, Blasco Ibáñez, Larra, Lugones, Rodó, Sarmiento, Valera, Zorrilla 17

    By country: Spain (122), Argentina (10), Nicaragua (8), Mexico (3), Cuba (2), Uruguay (2), the Philippines (2), Peru (1); the country is that of the author, so Juan Ruiz de Alarcón, born in Mexico, counts for Mexico. Every work is in the public domain both in Spain, where the author died before 1946, and in the United States, where it was published before 1931. The collection suits authorship attribution, the comparison of genres, of periods and of the Spanish of Spain and of America.

    The texts are the transcriptions of Project Gutenberg, cut to the text of the author: the title pages, the notes of the transcribers, the lists of other works and the advertisements, the tables of contents, the introductions, prologues and notes of the editors, the errata and the footnotes are removed, as well as the markup of the transcriptions (the underscores of italics, the images, the numbers of stanzas). The prologues, dedications and notes of the authors themselves stay. Works in several volumes are joined in one text. The years are those of the first publication (1605-1615 for the two parts of Don Quijote); the files of the collection are named after the numbers of the books of Project Gutenberg.

    The archive (19 MB) is kept in the repository of the library, is downloaded once into the data directory, verified against its SHA-256 checksum and extracted; the texts are read one at a time.

    Parameters¶

    Parameter Type Default Description
    data_dir str/Path DEFAULT_DATA_DIR.joinpath("texts") Path to the dataset directory; the data directory is described in Installation

    Attributes¶

    Attribute Type Description
    genres tuple[str] Tuple of genres: prose, poems, drama, publicism
    authors dict[str, str] Names of the authors by the names of their folders
    name str Name of the dataset, spanish_literature
    meta dict[str, str] Reference information: the source, the description, the author and the licence
    info dict[str, str] The name and the reference information in one dictionary
    data_dir Path Absolute path to the dataset directory
    filepath str Path to the archive of the dataset, None before the download

    The dataset iterates over its records as get_records() without filters: for record in sl goes over the works one at a time.

    Methods¶

    check_data¶

    Checks that the list of works and the file of every work are in place and returns True; a dataset that is not downloaded or misses a file raises DatasetNotFoundError. The other methods check it themselves.

    download¶

    Downloads the archive with checksum verification and extracts the files. A corrupted or replaced archive is removed and downloaded again in the same call; if the archive is there but some files are missing, it is extracted again. A repeated call downloads nothing.

    Parameter Type Default Description
    force bool False Download the dataset even if it is already downloaded

    Example

    from ests.datasets import SpanishLiterature

sl = SpanishLiterature()
sl.download()
sl.info
# {'name': 'spanish_literature',
#  'url': 'https://www.gutenberg.org',
#  'description': 'Spanish-language literature in the public domain',
#  'author': 'Shkarin S.S.',
#  'license': 'Public domain'}

    get_texts¶

    Extracts the texts (without headers) from the dataset.

    Parameter Type Default Description
    genre str None Genre: prose, poems, drama or publicism
    author str None Author: a substring of the name, regardless of case and accents
    country str None Country: a substring of the name in Spanish, regardless of case and accents
    year_from int None Earliest year of the first publication
    year_to int None Latest year of the first publication
    min_len int None Minimum text length (in characters)
    max_len int None Maximum text length (in characters)
    limit int None Number of texts

    The filters are combined; author="galdos" finds Benito Pérez Galdós, country="mexico" finds México. An unknown genre, an earliest year after the latest one, a length below one, a minimum length above the maximum one and a negative limit raise ParameterError.

    Example

    from ests.datasets import SpanishLiterature

sl = SpanishLiterature()
for text in sl.get_texts(genre="poems", author="Rosalía", limit=1):
    print(text[:107])
# Aunque no alcancen gloria,
#     Pensé escribiendo libro tan pequeño,
#     Son fáciles y breves mis canciones,

    get_records¶

    Extracts the records (with headers) from the dataset. Record fields: genre - genre, author - author, title - title, year_from and year_to - the years of the first publication, country - the country of the author in Spanish, text - text, file - path to the file. The records go by genre in the order of genres, within a genre by the folder of the author and by year.

    The parameters are the same as for get_texts.

    Example

    from ests.datasets import SpanishLiterature

sl = SpanishLiterature()

# The plays of the twentieth century
for record in sl.get_records(genre="drama", year_from=1900):
    print(record["title"], record["year_from"], record["author"])
# La señorita de Trevélez 1916 Carlos Arniches
# Los caciques 1920 Carlos Arniches
# Es mi hombre 1921 Carlos Arniches
# El marqués de Bradomín 1907 Ramón del Valle-Inclán
# Romance de lobos 1908 Ramón del Valle-Inclán
# Luces de bohemia 1920 Ramón del Valle-Inclán
# Divinas palabras 1920 Ramón del Valle-Inclán

    The spelling follows the edition

    The texts keep the spelling of the transcribed editions. 62 of them have the accents of their time (á as a preposition, fué, dió), 87 have the modern ones: the editions printed after the reforms of the Academy, and a dozen texts that the transcribers of Project Gutenberg modernized to the rules of 2010 (among them El castigo sin venganza, Las paredes oyen, Tirano Banderas, Luces de bohemia, Los caciques and Es mi hombre). Three novels come from editions of 1978-1982 with modern spelling (Arroz y tartana, La catedral, Juanita la Larga). Galdós has 12 texts of the first kind and 9 of the second. Since á and a are different words for a frequency list, stylometry over the most frequent words can separate the texts by their edition rather than by their author: remove the accent of the monosyllables (á, é, ó, ú, fué, dió, vió) before such a comparison. Abel Sánchez lacks most of the opening ¿ and ¡, as its edition does.

    Editions and contents

    Several works follow editions revised by their authors: the Opera Omnia of Valle-Inclán (1913-1924), the complete works of Pereda, Palacio Valdés and Blasco Ibáñez, the sixth edition of Reglas y consejos sobre investigación científica (1923), much expanded from the speech of 1897; the years stay those of the first publication. Some records are selections or single volumes: Tradiciones peruanas (27 tradiciones), Recuerdos del tiempo viejo I (the first of three volumes), El payador (the first and only published volume); the Entremeses are the eight of the collection of 1615, without the three of doubtful attribution. Novelas ejemplares go without La tía fingida. Two records hold two works each, as their titles say: Insolación y Morriña and Sonata de otoño. Sonata de invierno. In some plays of the Golden Age the square brackets of the editors remain around the stage directions and the names they added.
