    Lexical sophistication statistics¶

    LexicalStats(source, freq_dict=None, nlp=None)

    Lexical sophistication in the manner of TAALES (Kyle, Crossley and Berger, 2018) - how rare the words of a text are: statistics by a frequency dictionary and its unigram model, the shares of the frequency bands of a list of the most frequent lemmas and the lexical density. The statistics by the dictionary are computed when first read, so the bands and the lexical density need no dictionary data.

    Raises:

    • SourceTypeError - a dictionary of another class than that of the library or, without freq_dict_class, one without lookup and ipm or none at all; an analyze that does not give LexicalWord objects;
    • SourceError - a source without words.

    In esTS a source that is neither a string nor a Doc and a pipeline in nlp that is not a spaCy Language raise SourceTypeError too; a source without the parts of speech, a string longer than the max_length of the pipeline and a string with a character without a UTF-8 form SourceError; and the metrics by the dictionary without the dictionary downloaded DatasetNotFoundError.

    Unlike the lexical diversity metrics, which compare the words of a text with each other, here the words are compared with the frequencies of the language (TAALES). The data source is a text or a Doc object of the spaCy library.

    The class extends the LexicalStats of the anyTS core with the Spanish layer: the dictionary is the Google Books Ngram FreqDict (freq_dict_class), and the ranks of the lemmas and the size of the list (rank, top_size) come from the embedded list of its 10,000 most frequent lemmas (get_rank). Whether a word is a proper noun, and whether it is a content word, comes from the annotation, so the source has to be annotated: a string is parsed with es_core_news_sm or with the pipeline passed in nlp, without the parser, the lemmatizer and the named entities, and a Doc must carry the parts of speech (a morphologizer, or a tagger with an attribute_ruler). Numbers (2020, 5,5, 3.º) are not words. The lemmas come from lemma_key, the simplemma lemma in lower case, not from the model; a proper noun keeps its lower-case form. A content word is defined as in CohesionStats: one of CONTENT_UD_POS (nouns, proper nouns, adjectives, verbs, adverbs) and no demonstrative. The print_stats table has the descriptions of LEXICAL_STATS_DESC of ests.constants, with the range in years out of 40.

    The max_length of the pipeline is a million characters by default: split a longer text into parts, or raise max_length on a pipeline of your own and pass it in nlp.

    from ests import LexicalStats
from ests.datasets import FreqDict

FreqDict().download()
ls = LexicalStats("El gato estaba en la ventana y miraba a los pájaros")
ls.print_stats()

    Parameters¶

    Parameter Type Default Description
    source str / Doc - Data source
    freq_dict FreqDict None Frequency dictionary; FreqDict() from the default directory by default
    nlp Language None Pipeline of spaCy that parses a string; without it the model es_core_news_sm is loaded

    Attributes¶

    Attribute Type Description
    freq_dict FrequencyDictionary Frequency dictionary
    words, lemmas tuple[str] Words and their lemmas; the bands are counted by the lemmas
    keys tuple[str] Keys of the words in the dictionary
    entries tuple[FrequencyEntry / None] Entries of the dictionary, None for a word out of it
    ranks tuple[int / None] Ranks of the lemmas, None out of the list
    n_words, n_content_words, n_found int Number of words, of content words and of words found in the dictionary

    In esTS the dictionary is a FreqDict, and its entries are the named tuples Entry. The keys of the words in the dictionary (keys, dictionary_keys) are their lemmas, and a proper noun is looked up by its form when the dictionary has it (París as parís, not the verb parir) and by its lemma otherwise (Estados of Estados Unidos as estado, a sentence-initial Miró tagged PROPN as mirar). A word is looked up without the period of an abbreviation (Sr. as sr, pág. as página); an initial (D., J.) keeps it and is out of the dictionary.

    ls = LexicalStats("Miró desde París hacia los Estados Unidos")
ls.lemmas
ls.keys

    ('miró', 'desde', 'parís', 'hacia', 'el', 'estados', 'unidos')
('mirar', 'desde', 'parís', 'hacia', 'el', 'estado', 'unidos')

    Metrics¶

    Every metric is an attribute or a property of the object, and get_stats gives them all. A mean is over the words found in the dictionary, and without them it is nan.

    Metric Measures Range Higher means
    coverage Share of the words found in the dictionary 0-1 More words known to the dictionary
    mean_ipm, mean_ipm_content Mean frequency per million words of the words found, of the content words found Above 0 More frequent words
    mean_log_ipm, mean_log_ipm_content Mean decimal logarithm of the frequency of the words found, of the content words found Up to 6 More frequent words
    mean_range Mean range of the words found 0 and above Words used in more parts of the corpus
    mean_dispersion Mean dispersion of the words found That of the dictionary By the measure of the dictionary
    surprisal Mean surprisal of the words by the unigram model of the dictionary, in bits 0 and above Rarer words
    perplexity 2 to the power of the surprisal 1 and above Rarer words
    p_top1000, p_top2000, p_top5000, p_top10000 Share of the words with a lemma in the top 1000, 2000, 5000 and 10000 of the list 0-1, nan for a band beyond top_size More frequent words
    p_beyond_top10000 Share of the words with a lemma beyond the top 10000 0-1, nan for a list shorter than 10000 Rarer words
    lexical_density Share of the content words 0-1 More content words

    The metrics rely on two resources; always read the means by the dictionary next to coverage.

    Resource Metrics Availability
    FreqDict - 83,785 lemmas of Google Books Ngram (1980-2019) with ipm, range and dispersion coverage, mean_ipm*, mean_log_ipm*, mean_range, mean_dispersion, surprisal, perplexity downloaded once with FreqDict().download()
    The embedded list of the 10,000 most frequent lemmas of the same dictionary (ests/resources/google_books_top10000.txt, CC BY 3.0) p_top1000, p_top2000, p_top5000, p_top10000, p_beyond_top10000, band_coverage always

    Dictionary frequency¶

    coverage · mean_ipm · mean_ipm_content · mean_log_ipm · mean_log_ipm_content · mean_range · mean_dispersion · Google Books Ngram

    The metrics are computed from the FreqDict entries for the keys of the words (keys, entries): the means are taken over the words found in the dictionary, over all of them or only over the content words. The range is the number of years out of 40 (1980-2019) in which the lemma occurs in the books, and the dispersion is Juilland's D over the years, from 0 to 100. The mean frequency in ipm is sensitive to function words (el - 98,000 ipm, de - 71,000), so for comparing texts the log frequency or the mean over the content words is more convenient.

    Rare words lower the coverage and the log frequency and raise the surprisal and the share beyond the top 10000:

    from ests import LexicalStats

ls = LexicalStats("El felinólogo examinaba al minino con parsimonia")
ls.coverage, ls.mean_log_ipm, ls.surprisal, ls.p_beyond_top10000

    (0.8571428571428571, 2.3529196195453976, 13.70650419215419, 0.4285714285714286)

    Surprisal and perplexity¶

    surprisal · perplexity

    calc_surprisal(lemmas, freq_dict)

    The mean surprisal of the words by the unigram model of a frequency dictionary, in bits; a word out of the dictionary gets its lowest frequency min_ipm. The perplexity is 2 to its power.

    \[ \frac{1}{N} \sum_{w} -\log_2 \frac{\max(\textrm{ipm}_w, \textrm{min\_ipm})}{10^6} \]
    Parameter Type Default Description
    lemmas list[str] - Keys of the words in the dictionary
    freq_dict FrequencyDictionary - Frequency dictionary

    No words give nan; keys that are not a list of strings and a dictionary without ipm and min_ipm raise SourceTypeError, a lowest frequency that is not above zero ParameterError.

    In esTS the dictionary is a FreqDict with the lowest frequency of 0.1 ipm: a dictionary of another class raises SourceTypeError.

    from ests.datasets import FreqDict
from ests.lexical_stats import calc_surprisal

calc_surprisal(["gato", "estar", "felinólogo"], FreqDict())

    15.392368319318606

    Frequency bands¶

    p_top1000 · p_top2000 · p_top5000 · p_top10000 · p_beyond_top10000 · band_coverage(bands, unique)

    The share of the words whose lemma has a rank (rank) within the top \(N\) of the list, for the bounds 1000, 2000, 5000 and 10000 of FREQUENCY_BANDS; the share beyond the top 10000 is the rest, the words out of the list included. A band beyond the size of the list (top_size) is nan, and so is the share beyond the top 10000 for a list shorter than 10000.

    \[ \frac{\textrm{Number of words with a rank up to } N}{\textrm{Number of words}} \]

    This builds a frequency band profile in the manner of the Lexical Frequency Profile. The bands take the lower-case form of a proper noun: a proper noun whose form is a common lemma gets its rank (Gobierno 106), while the names and their inflected parts (Madrid, Estados) fall beyond the top 10000, so a text full of names has a greater share there.

    get_rank(lemma)

    The rank of a lemma by the embedded list, None for a lemma outside the list; the lemma may be in any case.

    Parameter Type Default Description
    lemma str - Lemma - the key of lemma_key 
    load_top_lemmas()

    The whole list from the file ests/resources/google_books_top10000.txt, derived from Google Books Ngram under CC BY 3.0: the rank of every lemma, starting from 1. The list holds the 10,000 most frequent lemmas of the frequency dictionary by decreasing frequency, the parts of speech of a lemma summed; it leaves out the proper nouns, the single letters other than the Spanish words a, e, o, u, y, á, é, ó, and the two-letter lemmas and Roman numerals that simplemma does not know (pp, xix), while the abbreviations of the references (cit) and the English words of the bibliographies (the) stay.

    from ests.lexical_stats import get_rank, load_top_lemmas

get_rank("el"), get_rank("gato"), get_rank("felinólogo")
len(load_top_lemmas())

    (1, 2851, None)
10000

    Lexical density¶

    lexical_density

    The share of the content words among all the words; a content word is one of CONTENT_UD_POS (nouns, proper nouns, adjectives, verbs, adverbs) and no demonstrative, as in CohesionStats, so the auxiliaries (es, ha) are not content words.

    \[ \frac{\textrm{Number of content words}}{\textrm{Number of words}} \]

    Methods¶

    band_coverage(bands, unique) gives the share of the words with a lemma in the top N of the list for every bound N of bands (FREQUENCY_BANDS by default), or with unique=True the share of the distinct lemmas. Bounds that are not a list of integers or a bound out of 1 and top_size() raise ParameterError.

    from ests import LexicalStats

ls = LexicalStats("El gato estaba en la ventana y miraba a los pájaros")
ls.band_coverage(unique=True)
ls.band_coverage(bands=(100, 500))

    {1000: 0.6666666666666666, 2000: 0.7777777777777778, 5000: 1.0, 10000: 1.0}
{100: 0.6363636363636364, 500: 0.7272727272727273}
