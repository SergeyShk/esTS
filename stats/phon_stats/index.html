    Phonostatistics¶

    PhonStats(source, words_extractor=None, window_len=3)

    The phonetic statistics of a text: its sounds by class, their clusters, sequences and repetitions, and its syllables. A sound is a letter of a word by default, or a sound of its transcription when a library gives one. The data source is a text or a Doc object of spaCy; the words are taken in lower case, and an extractor passed explicitly is used on the text of a Doc.

    Raises:

    • SourceTypeError - a source that is neither a string nor a Doc, an extractor of another type;
    • SourceError - a source without words;
    • ParameterError - a window that is not an integer of at least 2.

    Besides the errors above, a string with a character without a UTF-8 form raises SourceError from the Spanish tokenizer.

    The class extends the PhonStats of the anyTS core with the sounds of Spanish. The sounds are those of the transcription of the words (transcribe), not their letters: ch, ll, rr and qu are one sound, h and the u of que and gui none, and x two. The pronunciation is the one of the standard of Spain: yeísmo (ll and y are one sound) and distinción (c before e and i and z are θ, apart from s). The classes of the sounds are the constants of ests.constants in the table below. The syllables are those of syllabify, by the orthographic rules, and a hiatus is two vowels in two neighbouring syllables of a word (po-e-ta, dí-a, bú-ho). The words of a string are extracted by the Spanish WordsExtractor; without an extractor the words of a Doc come from its tokens. No trained model is needed. The print_stats table has the English descriptions of the core, anyts.constants.PHON_STATS_DESC.

    Class Sounds Constant
    Vowels a, e, i, o, u VOWEL_SOUNDS
    Sonorants m, n, ɲ (ñ), l, r (the tap and the trill are one sound) SONORANT_SOUNDS
    Voiced obstruents b (b, v), d, g, ʝ (y, ll) VOICED_SOUNDS
    Voiceless obstruents p, t, k (c, qu, k), f, θ (c, z), s, x (j, g), tʃ (ch) VOICELESS_SOUNDS 
    from ests import PhonStats

# The first stanza of Sonatina by Rubén Darío (Prosas profanas, 1896)

    "La princesa está triste... ¿qué tendrá la princesa?\n"
    "Los suspiros se escapan de su boca de fresa,\n"
    "que ha perdido la risa, que ha perdido el color.\n"
    "La princesa está pálida en su silla de oro,\n"
    "está mudo el teclado de su clave sonoro;\n"
    "y en un vaso olvidada se desmaya una flor."
)
ps = PhonStats(sonatina)
ps.print_stats()

    Transcription¶

    transcribe(word)

    Transcribes a word into its sounds, those PhonStats counts. The word is split into syllables (syllabify) and every syllable is read by the rules of the Spanish orthography; the characters that are no Spanish letters (digits, hyphens) are left out.

    Spelling Sound Example
    a, á; e, é; i, í; o, ó; u, ú, ü a, e, i, o, u canción - k a n θ i o n
    h none; hi before a vowel at the start of a syllable is ʝ ahora - a o r a, hielo - ʝ e l o
    ch tʃ hechizo - e tʃ i θ o
    c before e, i; z θ cena - θ e n a
    c elsewhere, qu before e, i, k k queso - k e s o
    g before e, i; j x gente - x e n t e
    g elsewhere, gu before e, i g guerra - g e r a
    ll; y before a vowel ʝ calle - k a ʝ e
    y at the end of a syllable i hoy - o i
    r, rr r perro - p e r o
    ñ ɲ niño - n i ɲ o
    v b vaca - b a k a
    x; x at the start of a word k s; s examen - e k s a m e n, xilófono - s i l o f o n o
    w u whisky - u i s k i

    The pronunciation is the one of the standard of Spain, with yeísmo and distinción. A rule reads the spelling of a word, not its history: the x of México is k s, as in examen.

    Parameter Type Default Description
    word str - Word

    Raises:

    • SourceTypeError - a word that is not a string.
    from ests.phon_stats import transcribe

transcribe("hechizo")
transcribe("guerrilla")
transcribe("examen")

('g', 'e', 'r', 'i', 'ʝ', 'a')
('e', 'k', 's', 'a', 'm', 'e', 'n')

    Metrics¶

    Every metric is an attribute of the object, computed when it is created, and get_stats gives them all.

    Metric Measures Range Higher means
    p_vowels Share of the vowels among the sounds of the classes 0-1, nan without sounds A more vocalic text
    p_sonorants Share of the sonorants among the sounds of the classes 0-1, nan without sounds A more sonorous text
    p_voiced Share of the voiced obstruents among the sounds of the classes 0-1, nan without sounds More voiced obstruents
    p_voiceless Share of the voiceless obstruents among the sounds of the classes 0-1, nan without sounds More voiceless obstruents
    consonant_vowel_ratio Ratio of the consonants to the vowels 0 and above, nan without vowels Denser consonants
    p_heavy_clusters Share of the clusters of three consonants or more 0-1, nan without clusters Heavier clusters
    p_hiatus Hiatuses of word_hiatus per word 0 and above, nan without sounds More vowels in a row
    cv_entropy Shannon entropy of the CV patterns of the words, in bits 0 and above, nan without sounds More varied shapes of the words
    hardness Voiceless obstruents to the vowels and the sonorants 0 and above, nan without vowels and sonorants A harder sound
    alliteration Repetition index of the consonants in windows of window_len words 0 and above, nan for a text shorter than the window or with no consonant shared by two words Consonants repeated closer together
    assonance Repetition index of the vowels in windows of window_len words 0 and above, nan for a text shorter than the window or with no vowel shared by two words Vowels repeated closer together
    p_open_syllables Share of the syllables that end in a vowel 0-1, nan without syllables More open syllables
    mean_syllable_len Mean length of a syllable in sounds 1 and above, nan without syllables Longer syllables

    The functions of the cards are imported from ests.phon_stats; they take words as strings and count over the sounds of their transcription.

    Shares of the classes of the sounds¶

    p_vowels · p_sonorants · p_voiced · p_voiceless

    The share of the sounds of every class - the vowels, the sonorants and the voiced and voiceless obstruents - among the sounds of all of them; a sound of skipped or of no class (a digit, a letter of another script) counts in none.

    \[ \frac{\textrm{Number of sounds of the class}}{\textrm{Number of vowels and consonants}} \]

    Ratio of the consonants to the vowels¶

    consonant_vowel_ratio

    The number of the consonants n_consonants to the number of the vowels n_vowels.

    \[ \frac{\textrm{Number of consonants}}{\textrm{Number of vowels}} \]

    Consonant clusters¶

    p_heavy_clusters · c_clusters

    calc_consonant_clusters(text)

    The distribution of the consonant clusters of the words by length, the attribute c_clusters. A cluster is a run of consonant sounds inside a word, across the syllables, a single consonant between vowels or at the edge of a word included: instrumento has the clusters n s t r (4), m (1) and n t (2). The digraphs are one sound (calle, perro, chico), and x is two (extra - k s t r). p_heavy_clusters is the share of the heavy clusters:

    \[ \frac{\textrm{Number of clusters of 3 consonants or more}}{\textrm{Number of clusters}} \]
    Parameter Type Default Description
    text list[str] - List of words

    Raises:

    • SourceTypeError - words that are not a list of strings.
    from ests.phon_stats import calc_consonant_clusters

calc_consonant_clusters(["instrumento", "calle"])

    {1: 3, 2: 1, 4: 1}

    Hiatuses¶

    p_hiatus

    calc_hiatus(text)

    The number of hiatuses: two vowels next to each other in two syllables of a word, as syllabify splits them - po-e-ta, dí-a, le-er, a-é-re-o (two). A silent h between the vowels does not break the hiatus (bú-ho, a-ho-ra), and the vowels of a diphthong are one syllable and no hiatus (cie-lo, ciu-dad). p_hiatus is the number of hiatuses of the text per word.

    Parameter Type Default Description
    text list[str] - List of words

    Raises:

    • SourceTypeError - words that are not a list of strings.
    from ests.phon_stats import calc_hiatus

calc_hiatus(["poeta", "búho", "cielo", "aéreo"])

    4

    Entropy of the CV patterns¶

    cv_entropy

    The Shannon entropy of the CV patterns of the words (cv_pattern), in bits, where \(P(p)\) is the share of the words with the pattern \(p\) among the words that have one.

    \[ -\sum_{p} P(p) \log_2 P(p) \]
    calc_cv_entropy(text)

    The entropy of the CV patterns of a list of words: the higher it is, the more varied the phonetic shape of the words of the text. Words without sounds (numbers) are left out; nan for a text without other words.

    cv_pattern(word)

    The CV pattern of a word or a syllable: the vowels are written V and the consonants C, over the sounds of the transcription - queso is CVCV, hora VCV, examen VCCVCVC. A syllable is read as a word of its own, so its initial x is s, as at the start of a word: xi is CV, while the syllable xi of México counts as CCV in PhonStats.

    Parameter Type Default Description
    text list[str] - List of words (calc_cv_entropy)
    word str - Word or syllable (cv_pattern)

    Raises:

    • SourceTypeError - words that are not a list of strings (calc_cv_entropy), a word that is not a string (cv_pattern).
    from ests.phon_stats import calc_cv_entropy, cv_pattern

cv_pattern("queso"), cv_pattern("examen")
calc_cv_entropy(["poeta", "búho", "cielo", "aéreo"])

    ('CVCV', 'VCCVCVC')
1.5

    Hardness¶

    hardness

    The number of the voiceless obstruents to the number of the vowels and the sonorants.

    \[ \frac{\textrm{Number of voiceless obstruents}}{\textrm{Number of vowels} + \textrm{Number of sonorants}} \]

    A text of p, t, k, s and θ sounds harder than a text of vowels and m, n, l, r.

    Alliteration and assonance¶

    alliteration · assonance

    calc_alliteration(text, window_len=3)

    calc_assonance(text, window_len=3)

    The alliteration and assonance indices of a list of words: the repetition index calc_repetition_index of anyts.phon_stats over the consonant and over the vowel sounds of the transcription of the words. The sounds count, not the letters: casa and queso repeat k, cena and casa do not; every vowel counts, stressed or not.

    Parameter Type Default Description
    text list[str] - List of words
    window_len int 3 Window in words (PHON_WINDOW_LEN)

    Raises:

    • SourceTypeError - words that are not a list of strings;
    • ParameterError - a window that is not an integer of at least 2.
    calc_repetition_index(words, window_len, features=None)

    The repetition index: the number of windows of window_len neighbouring words where a feature (a letter or a sound) occurs in two words or more, summed over the features, divided by the number expected if the words stood in random order. A window of shuffled words is a sample without replacement, so for a feature found in \(K\) of the \(N\) words, a window of \(w\) words holds it in two words or more with the hypergeometric probability

    \[ P = 1 - \frac{\binom{N-K}{w} + K \binom{N-K}{w-1}}{\binom{N}{w}} \]

    and the expected number is the sum of these probabilities over the features times the \(N - w + 1\) windows. Since the expectation comes from the text itself, the index shows whether the repetitions gather in neighbouring words, not how frequent a feature is: it averages to 1 over the orders of the words and is well above 1 when the repetitions come closer than chance. A word counts a feature once; the index is nan for a text shorter than the window or with no feature shared by two words.

    Parameter Type Default Description
    words list[str] / list[list[str]] - Features of every word: a string of its letters or a collection of its sounds
    window_len int - Window in words, at least 2
    features Collection[str] None Features to count, all of them by default

    Words that are not a list of strings or of collections of strings and features that are not a collection of strings raise SourceTypeError, and a window_len that is not an integer of at least 2 ParameterError.

    For words in random order the indices average to 1: the 150 books of the corpus of literature with their words shuffled give 0.98 to 1.02. On the books as written the index of alliteration is a little below 1 for verse and prose alike, 0.90 to 0.99 with a median of 0.95, and the index of assonance about 1, 0.99 to 1.01: over a whole book neighbouring words share a consonant a little less often than chance. The 4,259 sonnets give medians of 0.92 and 0.98, with a wide spread over such short texts (0.59 to 1.30 for alliteration). The repetitions of a poem are local, and the places where they gather are shown by the layer alliteration of the highlighting.

    from ests.phon_stats import calc_alliteration, calc_assonance

# Garcilaso de la Vega, Égloga III
words = "en el silencio sólo se escuchaba un susurro de abejas que sonaba".split()
calc_alliteration(words), calc_assonance(words)

    (1.0232558139534884, 1.115942028985507)

    Syllables¶

    p_open_syllables · mean_syllable_len

    The syllables are the sounds of every syllable of a word by syllable_sounds; a syllable without a sound of the classes (a number) is no syllable, in c_syllable_patterns too. An open syllable ends in a vowel by its CV pattern (cv_pattern), and the length of a syllable is the length of that pattern: the sounds of the classes, without skipped and the signs out of them.

    The sounds of a syllable are its transcription, the first syllable of a word read as the start of the word: the xi of xilófono is s i, the xi of México k s i.

    is_open_syllable(syllable)

    Checks whether a syllable is open: an open syllable ends in a vowel sound - ca, que, hoy (the final y is the vowel i); car and pan are closed. The syllable is read as a word of its own.

    Parameter Type Default Description
    syllable str - Syllable

    Raises:

    • SourceTypeError - a syllable that is not a string.
    from ests.phon_stats import is_open_syllable

is_open_syllable("hoy"), is_open_syllable("car")

    (True, False)
